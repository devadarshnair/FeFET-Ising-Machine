## 1. Measured per-step cost and what it does and does not tell us

Stage 1 cloud timing chunks (32 vCPU IN1, `scripts/testing/time_chunk.py`, 800-spin Max-Cut G1, 20 000 steps, one process each):

| Machine | us/step (cloud, Stage 1) | local (earlier P6.02 table) | cloud / local |
|---|---|---|---|
| ours (N = 1024 padded, kc2, I7/I10 on) | 184.24 | 287.5 | 0.641 |
| B1 native 3-bit, 1250 tile-ops per read | 2115.44 | 3132 | 0.675 |
| B3 DA | 47.45 | 58.5 | 0.811 |
| B2 (set-up dominated) | 40.99 | <= 4.6 | n/a |

**Caveat that changes the budget.** Those chunks ran at the smoke temperatures (T 3.0 -> 0.1 energy LSB). That is cold: ours skips most
field reads there (I10), so 184 us/step is not the cost of the registered percentile-rule schedules (P4.02 rule), where a flip is accepted
almost every step and every step reads the array. Measured with the runner under the registered schedule, ours costs about 3.6 ms/step at
1024 lanes with the golden `cim_field` (a w x n x n integer matrix-vector product per read) and B1 4.3 ms/step. Two things follow:

1. The runner replaces those products by exact incremental column sums (`scripts/testing/fastcim.py`: integer sums updated from the bits
   that changed). The fields are bit-identical (tests compare full machine traces with and without it: ours kc2/I7/I10, noise, MESA reloads,
   B1 row tiles). Ours then costs 290 us/step (Max-Cut), B1 1150, B3 90 locally.
2. The budget uses the runner's own local timings under the registered schedule (`plan.LOCAL_RUNNER_US`, per machine and family; H5 with the
   field wrapper `LOCAL_RUNNER_PERT_US`), mapped to the cloud by the mean local/cloud ratio of the three timed machines (x0.709).

| Cloud-equivalent us/step used | Max-Cut 800 spins | MaxSAT 316 spins | colouring 36 spins |
|---|---|---|---|
| ours (padded 1024 / 512 / 64 lanes) | 207 | 132 | 72 |
| B1 native | 816 | 285 | 93 |
| B3 | 64 | 57 | 43 |

The 800-vs-1024 effect: ours is charged for its padded lanes (1024 for the 800-spin G-set graphs, 512 for the 316-spin MaxSAT), B1 and B3
for the real spins; the table above is measured per family rather than extrapolated linearly in N. QKP (B2) is charged 0.3 s + 5 us per
proposal (the kernel is set-up dominated; <= 4.6 us). Golden (Python/numba) is the only engine in any sweep; Verilator and ngspice cost no
testing-phase CPU (RTL equivalence is a one-time check). Chunking rule: every job is planned at <= 25 min central (a x2 timing error
stays under 1 h); the longest planned job is 24.8 min.

## 2. Sweep design (from the DRAFT preregs) and projected CPU-hours

Design basis: 150 sweeps per run (steps = 150 x spins); H2 per arm = 100 test runs (10 instances x 10 seeds) + 64 tuning runs (8 configs x 4
disjoint instances x 2 seeds); ours simulates {restart none/DA/MESA/Kc2} x {I10, I7+I10} (the I10-free counters are reconstructed per
skipped step, exact at sigma = 0, tested) plus Kc2+I11 x {I10, I7+I10}; B1 four restart arms + bit-sliced; B3 one arm with the offset knob
inside its 8-config grid; B1 as published (untuned, test only); B2 on QKP (30 test + 10 tuning instances, 1e4 and 1e5 proposals); the MaxSAT
reference set R (20 seeds x 10x budget; colouring uses exact enumeration). H3: 20 trajectory configs for ours-best and 4 for B1 (its ADC
is exact from 6 bits, so the five ADC-bit values are cost-only re-pricing), 5 instances x 5 seeds, Max-Cut and MaxSAT; B3 reuses its H2
runs; the other 3580 cells are post-processing. H5: 24 points of which 22 are evaluable and 18 distinct simulations after the nominal run
is reused for the x1 / zero-drift rows, 5 arms (ours Kc2/MESA/none at I7, B1 MESA, ours Kc2 I7+I10), Max-Cut 3 instances, MaxSAT 5, 5 seeds.
Added 16 Nov: the T7 sign-magnitude extra arm (`ours.kc2.i7.sm`, all 17 simulated points, both families: 36 jobs, 720 runs, 3.6 CPU-h, reported
separately), B2 HyCiM filter on QKP (device-sigma and read-noise points only: 6 points x 3 instances x 5 seeds at 1e5 proposals, 6 jobs, 90 runs,
0.16 CPU-h at 60 us/proposal for the python-side true-feasibility tracking of the wrapped kernel; to be re-timed from the smoke) and two
record-only jobs that write the explicit not-evaluable record for the SS / FF corner rows (no corner card exists in the repo).

## 4. Compliance notes and flags

- Every job is one process of about 0.2 GB RSS (measured 0.17-0.21 GB; declared 0.4-0.9 GB in the TSV), resumable by done-marker, `timeout`
  45 min. The dispatcher (`payload/run_stage2.sh`) uses the Stage 1 design: done markers, `STAGE2_DEADLINE_MIN` (default 480), collect at the end.
- Not budgeted: rung 4d (I3, knapsack family only), rung 4e (I5, only if I11 fails) and rung 6 (TCAM-lite): off by default in the preregs.
  If enabled, each adds one ours arm per family (Max-Cut about 1 CPU-h at these speeds).
- Untimed: per-family scaling for ours/B1 at other sizes is from direct local measurement, but only on one host; the x1.5 scenario covers it.
- Everything here is a draft: the sweeps cannot be launched before the author tags the preregs (A8). The runner and evaluators exist
  (`scripts/testing/run_cell.py`, `evaluate_h2.py`, `evaluate_h3.py`, `evaluate_h5.py`, `--smoke`); the evaluators have not been run on any
  scored data. The payload must be rebuilt (`build_payload.sh`) from the commit that contains them before a Stage 2 launch.
